🔬 Research Projects

1. Unraveling the Complex Dynamics of the Human Microbiome in Health and Disease

Currently, I am deeply engaged in studying the complex and dynamic interactions within the human microbiome, focusing particularly on the skin microbiome and oral microbiome, both of which play critical roles in health and disease. In the context of atopic dermatitis (AD), I am investigating how shifts in the skin microbial community contribute to disease onset, progression, and flare-ups, including the role of microbial dysbiosis in modulating immune responses.
An integral part of this research is exploring how environmental toxins, such as air pollutants and heavy metals, affect the composition and function of skin-resident microbes. In addition, I am studying how lifestyle-related exposures, including e-cigarette smoking, impact the oral microbiome. E-cigarette aerosols contain compounds that can disrupt microbial balance in the oral cavity, leading to inflammation, changes in pH, and increased susceptibility to infection or disease. These microbial shifts may not only affect oral health but also have systemic consequences due to the close link between the oral microbiome and other body sites. I aim to uncover specific microbial signatures and functional markers that could serve as biomarkers of environmental exposure or early indicators of disease, leading to targeted prevention and personalized interventions.

2. Metabolomic Insights and Microbial Health Benefits

I am currently investigating how microbial metabolites influence human physiology, with a strong emphasis on their role in immune regulation, epithelial barrier integrity, and inflammatory processes. This is especially relevant in the context of atopic dermatitis, where microbial-derived metabolites may either mitigate or exacerbate inflammation and skin barrier dysfunction.
Another critical area of focus is examining how environmental pollutants—including microplastics, persistent organic pollutants (POPs), and airborne particulate matter—alter microbial metabolism and lead to systemic metabolic changes. I aim to study how such exposures may shift beneficial metabolite production or trigger alternative metabolic pathways that generate pro-inflammatory or toxic byproducts. In addition to analyzing metabolic changes at the single-metabolite level, I am working to integrate metabolic pathway-level insights that reflect systemic effects of microbial alterations. My goal is to define metabolic signatures that are consistently associated with environmental exposures and skin disorders, including AD, and to identify potential probiotic or dietary strategies that can restore metabolic homeostasis.

3. Leveraging Advanced Computational Approaches for Metabolomic, Transcriptomic, and Microbiome Data Integration

To fully understand how environmental exposures influence the microbiome and health outcomes, I am developing advanced computational pipelines to integrate multi-omics datasets, including metagenomics, meta transcriptomics, metabolomics, and Host transcriptomics sequencing data. This integrative approach is particularly important in complex diseases such as atopic dermatitis, where microbial dysbiosis, immune dysregulation, and environmental triggers converge.
My current efforts involve applying machine learning algorithms, network-based modeling, and statistical frameworks to identify microbial and metabolic patterns linked to environmental pollutant exposure and disease phenotypes. Specifically, I am analyzing how exposure to environmental toxins—including urban air pollution —modulates microbial gene expression and metabolite profiles, and how these in turn influence disease progression. To support high-throughput data analysis and interactive visualization, I am also developing GPU-accelerated platforms for real-time plotting of omics data. These tools will facilitate the interpretation of large-scale datasets, enabling faster discovery of microbial biomarkers, metabolic shifts, and host gene responses. Ultimately, this work aims to establish predictive models for early disease detection and personalized therapeutic strategies based on environmental exposure profiles.

4. Microbial Ecology Meets Precision Medicine

Bridging microbial ecology with data-driven health strategies to develop diagnostic and therapeutic tools. This involves discovering biomarkers for disease and pollutant exposure while crafting interventions to restore microbial and metabolic balance.
